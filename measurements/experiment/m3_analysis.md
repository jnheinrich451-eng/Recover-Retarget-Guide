## C. Readings for the chapter's analysis section (each sentence is backed by a row above)

1. **The camera result is a removal, not an addition.** The gain per pair tracks the backbone's own error (rho -0.91): where the backbone already followed the camera (265.4.002, 256.3.010, 431.5.009) the gain is small; where it ignored it (horsejump-high 21.7 to 5.7, 252.2.007 10.0 to 1.9, 404.1.006 10.0 to 1.8) the gain is large. The correction supplies the camera the backbone lacks and leaves alone what it has. The backbone's error itself grows with the reference's rotation (rho +0.44): an image-to-video model left alone tends to keep the camera still.
2. **The object loss on humans is the camera channel working without an object channel.** With the camera held to the reference, the subject's on-screen path is moved by the camera; the human arm renders no object, so nothing moves the subject's body with it, and the motion-only path error grows with how much the camera turns (rho +0.52 on the 12 humans; the three vehicles, whose arm renders the object, win 3 of 3). The tendency is not a law: 191 (8.4 deg) wins, 431 (1.7 deg) is the largest loss. On 431 the backbone kept the subject's own path near the reference's and the guided run did not; the blind verdict saw a correct camera carrying the subject, the metric saw the subject's path relative to its own first frame.
3. **The appearance cost is not explained by coverage or by the camera's turn.** CLIP-IQA falls on 13 of 15 pairs, but the size of the fall tracks neither the render coverage at the last frame (rho +0.05) nor the reference camera's rotation (rho +0.12) nor the object loss (rho -0.08). The cost is therefore roughly uniform across pairs rather than proportional to how much the correction had to do: it reads as a fixed price of correcting toward a rendered target at all (the render's point-splat texture and its seams enter the latent at every corrected step), not as a price of camera motion. The blind verdicts' 'distorted in later frames' is consistent with coverage decay in the frames the metric averages over, but the metric does not resolve it at n = 15. The geometry refresh that Chapter 3 designs and does not build is the designed remedy for the late-frame part; the uniform part would need a better render, not more of it.
4. **The depth-gate flag predicts nothing because nothing downstream reads it.** The gate compares the reconstructed object's depth with the anchor's, which the human arm never uses in the loop; it is a record of reconstruction quality, not a predictor of the object result (table above).
5. **The eye-versus-metric disagreement is one mechanism, not seven accidents.** All seven disagreements are human pairs where the camera is held and the subject is carried along: the eye reads 'the subject moves with the scene as in the reference' as faithful; the metric reads the subject's path relative to its own first frame against the reference's and finds it longer or differently shaped. Neither is wrong about what it measures; the chapter names the metric's definition wherever it reports the object result.
7. **The two object metrics disagree with each other on the two by-eye pairs, in opposite directions.** On 431.5.009 the direction cosine of the subject's motion goes from −1.00 (backbone: the subject moves against the reference's direction) to +1.00 (ours: with it), while the motion-only path error goes from 4.7 to 62.4 px: the direction metric sides with the eye, the path metric with the magnitude (ours walks far, the backbone barely moves, the reference's subject net displacement is small on screen). On 217.0.006 the reverse: the path error improves (11.0 to 8.9) while the direction cosine goes from +0.88 to −0.85, because the anchor's car faces the camera and ours drives toward it where the reference drives away; the eye called the movements right and did not read the sign. Across the set the direction cosine improves or holds on 13 of 15 pairs (median +0.77 to +0.99 in the main table). The chapter reports the object result on the registered primary and names the direction cosine beside it as the exploratory metric that reads the subject's heading rather than its path length; the two together say the guided subject moves the right way and too far, which is what a held camera without an object channel produces.
6. **What would change the object result** (stated as design, not as a claim): an object render for the human arm, which the vehicle arm already has and which wins 3 of 3; the vehicle count is three, so this is a direction, not a finding.
